## Deliverable 1: Executive Summary

### 1.1 Corrected Concept Overview

**KINETIX TWIN** is a tabletop spatial physics laboratory for Snap SPECS that lets learners, educators, and engineers run a controlled, side-by-side counterfactual experiment in 3D space on a physical desk. When the user configures a launch vector and triggers an experiment, two isolated physics simulations execute simultaneously within the same spatial coordinate frame using native [Lens Studio `Physics.WorldComponent` instances](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/physics/physics-component):

1. **The Idealized Reference Twin (`World_Ideal_Alpha`, Cyan Wireframe/Fresnel Ghost):** Models textbook frictionless, elastic mechanics ($\mu = 0$, rolling friction $= 0$, spinning friction $= 0$, effective restitution $e_{\text{eff}} = 1.0$, linear/angular damping $= 0$).
2. **The Dissipative Real-World Twin (`World_Real_Beta`, Solid Amber/Metallic Body):** Models configurable surface friction ($\mu > 0$), rolling resistance, inelastic restitution ($e_{\text{eff}} < 1.0$), and rotational energy partitioning under identical initial geometry and impulse magnitude.

Instead of placing 2D graphs on a floating screen that splits visual attention, **KINETIX TWIN** renders key physical quantities—applied impulse ($\vec{J}$), moment-arm offset ($\vec{r}$), angular impulse ($\Delta \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J}$), normal contact response ($\hat{n}$ scaled by scalar normal impulse $J_n$), stroboscopic equal-time breadcrumb markers, and spatial divergence ($|\Delta \vec{x}|$)—directly on the 3D apparatus at real tabletop scale.

### 1.2 Principal Audit Corrections to the Original Proposal

A rigorous technical and scientific audit of the original `Kinetix twin.md` proposal against official Snap, SPECS, CLAD, and Devpost documentation revealed seven critical defects that would have undermined scientific validity, broken runtime execution, or caused schedule failure if left uncorrected:

1. **Correction of Confounded Experimental Variables (Scientific Integrity):** The original proposal applied a center-of-mass impulse (`addForce`) to the Ideal Twin while applying an off-center impulse (`addForceAt`) *and* adding surface friction/inelasticity to the Real Twin simultaneously. Changing both the moment arm $\vec{r}$ and surface dissipation $\mu, e$ in the same run makes it scientifically impossible to attribute trajectory divergence to either cause. The revised design enforces **Single-Variable Isolation** across two explicit experimental modes:
   - **Mode A (Surface Dissipation Isolation — Primary MVP):** Both twins receive the exact same center-of-mass impulse ($\vec{r} = \vec{0}$) on the inclined ramp/tabletop; only `Physics.Matter` friction and restitution differ between `World_Ideal_Alpha` and `World_Real_Beta`.
   - **Mode B (Off-Center Angular Impulse Isolation):** Both twins share low-dissipation surface settings, but the Ideal Twin receives a central impulse ($\vec{r} = \vec{0}$) while the Rotational Twin receives the same impulse magnitude $|\vec{J}|$ at a user-selected offset $\vec{r} \neq \vec{0}$, isolating how translational kinetic energy ($K_{\text{trans}} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$) partitions into rotational kinetic energy ($K_{\text{rot}} = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$).
2. **Correction of Torque ($\vec{\tau}$) vs. Angular Impulse ($\Delta \vec{L}$) Terminology & Units:** The original proposal wrote $\vec{\tau}_{\text{imp}} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J}$ and labeled the resulting vector "Torque ($\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$)" interchangeably. Because the launch uses `Physics.ForceMode.Impulse`, the applied vector is linear impulse $\vec{J} = \int \vec{F}\,dt$, and its cross product with the moment arm $\vec{r}$ is **Angular Impulse** ($\Delta \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J} = \int \vec{\tau}\,dt$), which produces an instantaneous change in angular momentum $\Delta \vec{\omega} = I^{-1}\Delta \vec{L}$. Furthermore, Lens Studio's physics engine operates in **centimeter-kilogram-second (CGS-hybrid) units** where gravity defaults to $g = 980\text{ cm/s}^2$, velocity is in $\text{cm/s}$, mass is in $\text{kg}$, and [`Contact.impulse` is documented in $\text{kg}\cdot\text{cm/s}$](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/api/lens-scripting/classes/Built-In.Contact.html) ($1\text{ N}\cdot\text{s} = 100\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm/s}$). All equations and UI readouts are now dimensionally consistent.
3. **Correction of `addForceAt` Coordinate Frame Semantics:** According to the [Lens Studio `BodyComponent` API documentation](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/api/lens-scripting/classes/Built-In.BodyComponent.html), `addForceAt(force: vec3, offset: vec3, mode: ForceMode)` applies force at a point **offset from the object's origin** (whereas `addRelativeForceAt` applies force relative to local rotation). Passing an unvalidated world-space position or misinterpreting local vs. world axes will produce erratic spin axes. The build plan mandates an explicit Phase 6/8 coordinate-frame calibration check in Preview before building the 3D cross-product triad.
4. **Correction of Flawed Cross-World Energy Accounting:** The original proposal computed dissipated energy by subtracting the Real Twin's instantaneous total energy from the Ideal Twin's instantaneous total energy ($\Delta E_{\text{diss}}(t) = \max(0, E_{\text{ideal}}(t) - E_{\text{real}}(t))$). Because numerical integration, constraint stabilization, and collision timing differ slightly between two separate physics worlds, cross-world subtraction introduces phantom energy artifacts. The revised specification tracks **Internal Per-World Mechanical Energy Conservation** relative to each world's own initial post-launch mechanical energy $E_0 = K_{\text{trans}}(0^+) + K_{\text{rot}}(0^+) + U_{\text{grav}}(0^+)$, converting CGS-hybrid quantities ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{cm}^2/\text{s}^2 \times 10^{-4} = \text{Joules}$) accurately.
5. **Correction of Contact Telemetry Assumptions (`contact.impulse` is a Scalar):** The [Lens Studio `Contact` class API](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/api/lens-scripting/classes/Built-In.Contact.html) exposes only four read-only properties: `distance: number`, `impulse: number`, `normal: vec3`, and `position: vec3`. It does **not** expose a 3D impulse vector or a direct frictional impulse vector. Therefore, only the normal impulse vector $\vec{J}_n = J_n \hat{n}$ is directly measured by the engine; any tangential friction vector must either be omitted in the MVP or clearly labeled as an **estimated slip-direction indicator** derived from tangential relative velocity $-\hat{v}_{\parallel}$.
6. **Correction of Swept-Sphere Prediction (`sphereCast` is Linear, Not Parabolic):** [`Physics.Probe.sphereCast`](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/physics/raycast) sweeps a sphere along a straight-line segment between `rayStart` and `rayEnd`. It does **not** integrate gravity ($g = -980\text{ cm/s}^2$) or surface friction along a curved trajectory. In the revised design, `sphereCast` is used strictly for straight-line initial clearance/aiming direction on flat surfaces, or replaced by an analytical kinematic arc preview that honestly communicates idealized pre-collision projection.
7. **Correction of Toolchain & Scope Risks (Lens Studio 5.24.1 Sign-In Gate & Over-Scoped Features):** A live community thread on the [Hackathon Discussion Forum](https://learn-specs.devpost.com/forum_topics) (`"Lens Studio 5.24.1: CLAD blocked by SPECS developer sign-in"`) and the [Lens Studio v5.24.1 Release Notes](https://ar.snap.com/lens-studio-v5) confirm that Lens Studio 5.24.0/5.24.1 introduced a mandatory `SPECS DevPortal` account sign-in gate where unverified accounts restrict features. This plan recommends **[Lens Studio v5.23.2](https://ar.snap.com/download/v5-23-2)** (released August 17, 2026) as the primary trouble-free version for solo builders without a verified SPECS hardware developer account, while providing exact verification steps if 5.24.1 is used. Furthermore, high-risk features from the original proposal—live camera ML material friction recovery (which is physically impossible from RGB pixels and unsupported for still images in Preview per [Camera Module docs](https://developers.snap.com/spectacles/about-spectacles-features/apis/camera-module)), custom optical waveguide shader graphs, 3 separate laboratories, and kinematic palm deflection—are demoted from the critical path so a solo beginner can ship a rock-solid, scientifically valid MVP first.

### 1.4 Recommended MVP Summary

The **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)** is a single, self-contained **Tabletop Ramp & Runout Counterfactual Workbench (Lab 1)** anchored at a comfortable desk position in front of the user, featuring:
- **Dual Isolated Physics Worlds (`World_Ideal_Alpha` and `World_Real_Beta`)** with explicit `Physics.WorldSettingsAsset` configurations (`simulationRate = 120`, $g = (0, -980, 0)\text{ cm/s}^2$).
- **Two Scientifically Controlled Experiment Modes:** Mode A (*Friction & Restitution Isolation* on the ramp/runout) and Mode B (*Off-Center Angular Impulse Isolation* on the flat deck).
- **Three Calibrated Educational Material Presets (`Physics.Matter`):** Low-Friction Ice Preset, Medium-Friction Steel Preset, and High-Friction Dissipative Rubber Preset.
- **Desk-Edge Control Bezel (`/specs-build-ui`):** Clean world-space UIKit buttons for Mode selection, Material selection, Launch Impulse preset/aiming, Slow-Motion toggle, and deterministic Reset.
- **Pre-Allocated 3D Vector & Trajectory Telemetry:** Transform-scaled 3D arrows for Applied Impulse ($\vec{J}$), Moment Arm ($\vec{r}$), Angular Impulse ($\Delta \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J}$), and Contact Normal Impulse ($\vec{J}_n$), plus pooled stroboscopic breadcrumb markers and an internal per-world energy & divergence readout pill.
- **Interactive Hypothesis Target Ring:** A draggable `SIK` `Interactable` + `InteractableManipulation` ring with an intangible `Physics.ColliderComponent` (`intangible = true`, `onOverlapEnter`) that evaluates the learner's landing prediction in centimeters.
- **Three Repeatable LEAF Automated Test Scenarios:** Verifying (1) Material preset switching, (2) Ideal vs. Real spatial divergence under high friction, and (3) Reset determinism.

All secondary features—Labs 2 and 3, procedural `MeshBuilder` ribbons, custom shader graphs, camera surface heuristics, and audio synthesis—are strictly gated behind MVP completion (Phases 10–12) with zero risk to the core submission.

### 3.2 Detailed Audit Analysis by Technical Domain (Sections 6.A – 6.J)

#### A. Physics Engine and Isolated Worlds (`Physics.WorldComponent`)
1. **Verified Multi-World Architecture:** Per [Snap's Physics Assets and Components documentation](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/physics/physics-component), Lens Studio has an implicit root physics world (`updateOrder = 0`), and developers can create multiple independent `Physics.WorldComponent` instances in the same scene. Any `Physics.ColliderComponent` or `Physics.BodyComponent` placed as a descendant of a `SceneObject` bearing a `Physics.WorldComponent` belongs exclusively to that world and will never collide with objects in another `WorldComponent`.
2. **WorldSettings Assignment:** The default root world configuration **cannot be customized** unless a `Physics.WorldSettingsAsset` is created in the Asset Browser and linked either to the Scene asset's `Physics Root Settings` field or directly to each `WorldComponent.worldSettings` property (or created in script via `Physics.WorldSettingsAsset.create()`). Therefore, KINETIX TWIN must explicitly assign two distinct `WorldSettingsAsset` assets—`IdealWorldSettings` and `RealWorldSettings`—to `World_Ideal_Alpha` and `World_Real_Beta`, both configured with `gravity = new vec3(0, -980, 0)`, `simulationRate = 120`, and `relativeSpeedLimit = 0.5` (which prevents a body from moving more than half its size in a single step, eliminating high-speed tunneling).
3. **Deterministic Synchronization & Reset:** To ensure a fair comparison, `World_Ideal_Alpha` (`updateOrder = -1`) and `World_Real_Beta` (`updateOrder = -1` or `0`) must be initialized with identical local apparatus geometry. On every reset, the controller must:
   - Reset `slowDownTime = 1.0` and `slowDownStep = 1.0` on both `WorldSettingsAsset` instances.
   - Call `bodyComponent.clearMotion()` on both dynamic bodies (which clears all linear/angular velocities and accumulated forces per the [`BodyComponent` API](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/api/lens-scripting/classes/Built-In.BodyComponent.html)) and explicitly set `body.velocity = vec3.zero()` and `body.angularVelocity = vec3.zero()`.
   - Restore identical local starting positions and `quat.quatIdentity()` rotations relative to each world's root transform.
4. **Spatial Legibility Without Z-Fighting:** If two opaque spheres of radius $3.0\text{ cm}$ start at the exact same world coordinates, their surfaces overlap 100%. We resolve this cleanly by placing the two worlds on **parallel twin tracks separated by $\Delta Z = 8\text{ cm}$ in depth (back lane = Ideal Cyan Ghost, front lane = Real Amber Solid) with a shared translucent comparison bridge**, OR superimposed with the Ideal Twin using an unlit additive wireframe/Fresnel material (`renderOrder = 10`) and slightly larger radius ($3.05\text{ cm}$). In fact, a **Side-by-Side Parallel Twin Track ($\Delta Z = 8\text{ cm}$ depth offset along the same $X$-axis ramp)** is far clearer for human stereo vision on SPECS waveguides and eliminates all z-fighting without needing complex shaders! We adopt the **Parallel Twin-Lane Workbench ($\Delta Z = 8\text{ cm}$ separation along the $Z$ axis, moving left-to-right along the $X$ axis)** so the user views both balls racing side-by-side from left ($-X$) to right ($+X$) across their desk.

#### C. Fair Comparison Between Ideal and Real Simulations
To make the counterfactual comparison scientifically valid, every experiment must hold initial geometry, mass ($m = 1.0\text{ kg}$), radius ($R = 3.0\text{ cm}$), gravity ($g = 980\text{ cm/s}^2$), and launch impulse magnitude ($|\vec{J}|$) **strictly constant** across both worlds while varying **one independent variable at a time**:
1. **Experiment Mode 1 — Ramp & Runout Surface Dissipation (Independent Variable: Surface Matter $\mu, e$):**
   - **Constant Conditions:** Both spheres start at rest at the top of identical $25^\circ$ ramps (or receive identical horizontal center-of-mass impulse $\vec{J}$ with $\vec{r} = \vec{0}$).
   - **Manipulated Variable:** `World_Ideal_Alpha` uses `IdealMatter` ($\mu = 0, \text{rollingFriction} = 0, e_{\text{eff}} = 1.0$). `World_Real_Beta` uses the selected preset (`Ice`, `Steel`, or `Rubber` with $\mu > 0, \text{rollingFriction} > 0, e_{\text{eff}} < 1.0$).
   - **Observable Phenomenon & Valid Conclusion:** On the ramp, static/sliding friction in the Real Twin exerts a torque at the contact point that converts translational potential energy into rotational kinetic energy ($K_{\text{rot}} = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$), so the rolling Real Sphere accelerates at $a \approx \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + 2/5} = \frac{5}{7}g\sin\theta$, whereas the frictionless Ideal Sphere slides without rolling at $a = g\sin\theta$! Then, on the flat runout and bumper impact, rolling resistance and inelastic restitution ($e < 1.0$) decelerate the Real Twin while the Ideal Twin conserves mechanical energy.
2. **Experiment Mode 2 — Off-Center Impact & Angular Impulse Partitioning (Independent Variable: Moment Arm Offset $\vec{r}$):**
   - **Constant Conditions:** Both spheres start at rest on the flat horizontal deck with identical low-friction surface settings and receive the exact same linear impulse vector $\vec{J}$.
   - **Manipulated Variable:** `World_Ideal_Alpha` applies $\vec{J}$ at the center of mass ($\vec{r} = \vec{0}$ via `addForce`). `World_Real_Beta` applies $\vec{J}$ at a user-selected radial offset ($\vec{r} \neq \vec{0}$ via `addForceAt`), generating angular impulse $\Delta \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J}$.
   - **Observable Phenomenon & Valid Conclusion:** The learner observes the 3D Right-Hand-Rule triad ($\vec{r}$, $\vec{J}$, and $\Delta \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J}$), sees the Real Twin acquire immediate 3D spin $\vec{\omega}$ around the axis of $\Delta \vec{L}$, and observes how surface friction couples with topspin/backspin/sidespin to curve or alter post-bounce trajectories.

#### D. Friction and Restitution (`Physics.Matter` Combination Rules)
Per the [Lens Studio Physics Matter documentation](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/physics/physics-component):
- **Effective Friction Product Rule:** $\mu_{\text{eff}} = \mu_{\text{colliderA}} \times \mu_{\text{colliderB}}$. Default friction is $0.5$, yielding $0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.25$. Values from $0$ to $10$ are permitted.
- **Effective Bounciness Product Rule:** $e_{\text{eff}} = e_{\text{colliderA}} \times e_{\text{colliderB}}$. Crucially, **`dynamicBounciness` defaults to `0.0`** on dynamic bodies, while `staticBounciness` defaults to `1.0` on static colliders. If a developer leaves `dynamicBounciness` at its default `0.0` on the sphere, $e_{\text{eff}} = 0.0 \times 1.0 = 0.0$, and the sphere will never bounce regardless of how high `staticBounciness` is set on the floor!
- **Calibrated KINETIX TWIN `Physics.Matter` Table:**
  To make the UI presets transparent and mathematically exact, we set the static ramp/tabletop collider in `World_Real_Beta` (`Real_SurfaceMatter`) to unit neutral values ($\mu = 1.0$, `staticBounciness = 1.0`, `dynamicBounciness = 1.0`, `rollingFriction = 0.0`, `spinningFriction = 0.0`) so that the sphere's assigned `Physics.Matter` directly controls the collision product:

| Preset Name | Assigned To | `friction` ($\mu$) | `rollingFriction` | `spinningFriction` | `dynamicBounciness` ($e$) | `staticBounciness` | Resulting Effective Collision ($\mu_{\text{eff}}, e_{\text{eff}}$ against `Real_SurfaceMatter`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`IdealMatter`** | `World_Ideal_Alpha` Sphere & Ramp/Table | `0.00` | `0.00` | `0.00` | `1.00` | `1.00` | $\mu_{\text{eff}} = 0.00,\; e_{\text{eff}} = 1.00$ (Zero sliding/rolling loss, 100% elastic bounce) |
| **`Real_SurfaceMatter`** | `World_Real_Beta` Ramp, Table & Bumper | `1.00` | `0.00` | `0.00` | `1.00` | `1.00` | Unit multiplier baseline ($\mu_{\text{surf}} = 1.0,\; e_{\text{surf}} = 1.0$) |
| **`IceMatter` (Preset 1)** | `World_Real_Beta` Sphere (when Ice active) | `0.03` | `0.005` | `0.005` | `0.92` | `0.92` | $\mu_{\text{eff}} = 0.03,\; e_{\text{eff}} = 0.92$ (Near-ideal glide, minimal divergence) |
| **`SteelMatter` (Preset 2)** | `World_Real_Beta` Sphere (when Steel active) | `0.35` | `0.06` | `0.05` | `0.68` | `0.68` | $\mu_{\text{eff}} = 0.35,\; e_{\text{eff}} = 0.68$ (Clear slip-to-roll transition, moderate bounce) |
| **`RubberMatter` (Preset 3)** | `World_Real_Beta` Sphere (when Rubber active) | `0.85` | `0.20` | `0.15` | `0.45` | `0.45` | $\mu_{\text{eff}} = 0.85,\; e_{\text{eff}} = 0.45$ (Rapid spin-up, strong rolling deceleration & damped bounce) |

*Note on Rubber Restitution:* In the original proposal, Rubber was given $e = 0.80$ while Steel had $e = 0.65$, which made Rubber *more* elastic in bounce while simultaneously claiming higher dissipative loss. Setting `RubberMatter` (Damped High-Grip Elastomer) to $\mu = 0.85, e = 0.45$ ensures monotonic progression from Ice (low dissipation) -> Steel (medium dissipation) -> Damped Rubber (high dissipation), making visual comparisons unmistakable.

#### E. Collision Events and Contact Telemetry
Per [Snap's Collision and Overlap documentation](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/physics/collision-and-overlap) and [`Contact` class reference](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/api/lens-scripting/classes/Built-In.Contact.html):
- Only dynamic `BodyComponent` instances generate `onCollisionEnter`, `onCollisionStay`, and `onCollisionExit` events.
- Inside the callback `(e) => { const collision = e.collision; }`, `collision.contacts` is an array of `Contact` objects of length `collision.contactCount`.
- Each `Contact` exposes:
  - `contact.position` (`vec3`): World-space contact point on the hit collider.
  - `contact.normal` (`vec3`): Unit surface normal on the hit collider pointing outward.
  - `contact.distance` (`number`): Separation/penetration distance along the normal.
  - `contact.impulse` (`number`): **Scalar** normal impulse ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{cm/s}$) applied along the normal in response to the collision.
- **Memory & Performance Guardrail:** Never instantiate `SceneObject` or `MeshBuilder` instances inside `onCollisionEnter` or `onCollisionStay`. Instead, pre-allocate one reusable **Contact Normal Arrow (`SceneObject`)** in the scene hierarchy. When `onCollisionEnter` fires with `contact.impulse > threshold` (e.g., $> 25\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm/s}$), snap the pre-allocated arrow's world position to `contact.position`, orient its $+Y$ axis along `contact.normal`, scale its length proportionally to `Math.min(18.0, contact.impulse * scaleFactor)`, and display its scalar magnitude in $\text{N}\cdot\text{s}$ (`0.01 * contact.impulse`).

#### F. Energy Accounting
To avoid misleading cross-world subtraction, **KINETIX TWIN** computes internal mechanical energy inside each world independently on every `UpdateEvent`:
1. Immediately after launch impulse application (at frame $t = 0^+$, or at release from the top of the ramp at height $y_0$), record each body's initial total mechanical energy relative to the lowest tabletop reference plane $y_{\text{ref}}$:
   $$E_{\text{initial}} = K_{\text{trans}}(0^+) + K_{\text{rot}}(0^+) + U_{\text{grav}}(0^+)$$
2. At each subsequent frame $t$:
   - $K_{\text{trans}}(t) = 10^{-4} \cdot \frac{1}{2} m |\vec{v}(t)|^2\text{ (J)}$
   - $K_{\text{rot}}(t) = 10^{-4} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{2}{5} m R^2\right) |\vec{\omega}(t)|^2\text{ (J)}$
   - $U_{\text{grav}}(t) = 10^{-4} \cdot m g \max(0, y(t) - y_{\text{ref}})\text{ (J)}$
   - $E_{\text{mech}}(t) = K_{\text{trans}}(t) + K_{\text{rot}}(t) + U_{\text{grav}}(t)$
   - **Estimated Cumulative Energy Dissipated:** $E_{\text{diss}}(t) = \max(0, E_{\text{initial}} - E_{\text{mech}}(t))$, or as a percentage:
     $$\text{Dissipated \%} = \text{clamp}\left(\frac{E_{\text{initial}} - E_{\text{mech}}(t)}{E_{\text{initial}}} \times 100,\; 0\%,\; 100\%\right)$$
3. **Honest Telemetry Labeling:** Because `Physics.Matter.rollingFriction`, `spinningFriction`, `damping`, and discrete solver stabilization are numerical approximations rather than thermodynamic heat sensors, the UI readout labels this metric: **`Est. Mechanical Energy Loss: XX% (Spin: YY% | Trans: ZZ%)`**.

#### G. Collision Prediction, Target Rings, and Bullet-Time
1. **Hypothesis Target Ring (`onOverlapEnter`):**
   - Per [Collision and Overlap — OnOverlapEnter/Stay/Exit Events](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/physics/collision-and-overlap), a volume trigger should use a `Physics.ColliderComponent` with **`collider.intangible = true`** so dynamic bodies pass through it without physical deflection.
   - Its `overlapFilter` defaults to `includeDynamic = true`, `includeStatic = true`, and `includeIntangible = false`. To ensure the Hypothesis Target Ring only triggers when the **Real Twin Sphere** enters it (and does not trigger by overlapping the static table or ramp!), we must set:
     - Place `HypothesisTargetRing` as a child inside `World_Real_Beta` (so it only checks overlaps within `World_Real_Beta`), **AND**
     - Set `ringCollider.overlapFilter.includeStatic = false`, `ringCollider.overlapFilter.includeDynamic = true`, and optionally `ringCollider.overlapFilter.onlyColliders = [realSphereBody]`!
     - **Why this audit finding is critical:** In the original proposal, `overlapFilter.includeStatic` was left at its default `true`. Because the ring rests on the static tabletop collider, `onOverlapEnter` would have fired immediately against the table floor rather than waiting for the sphere! Setting `includeStatic = false` and `onlyColliders = [realSphereBody]` prevents false triggers against the table.
2. **Per-World Slow Motion (`slowDownTime`):**
   - `IdealWorldSettings.slowDownTime` and `RealWorldSettings.slowDownTime` must always be updated together in lockstep (e.g., both set to `4.0` when slow-motion inspection is active, and both restored to `1.0` when normal speed resumes or when Reset is clicked).

### 4.2 The User Problem (Section 7.2)
Learners studying classical mechanics consistently struggle with three conceptual transitions:
1. **Connecting Idealized Equations to Real Dissipative Motion:** Textbook formulas initially assume frictionless point masses ($\mu = 0, e = 1.0$) where a block or sphere sliding down an incline accelerates at $g\sin\theta$ and conserves 100% of its mechanical energy. When learners observe a real sphere rolling down a ramp and decelerating across a desk, three effects happen at once: static/kinetic friction creates torque that spins up the sphere ($K_{\text{rot}} = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$), rolling resistance opposes motion, and inelastic impacts dissipate energy ($e < 1.0$). Without a side-by-side zero-loss baseline running under identical initial conditions, learners cannot visually separate how much motion changed due to rotational energy partitioning versus frictional dissipation.
2. **Visualizing 3D Vector Cross Products and Moment Arms:** When an impulse $\vec{J}$ strikes a rigid body at an offset $\vec{r}$ from its center of mass, the resulting angular impulse $\Delta \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J}$ points perpendicular to the plane of $\vec{r}$ and $\vec{J}$. On flat paper or 2D screens, learners routinely confuse the direction of force with the axis of rotation.
3. **Inspecting Transient Collision Dynamics:** Rigid-body impacts occur over brief contact intervals. Without synchronized slow-motion dilation and spatial contact-normal indicators, a learner only sees the before and after states, never the normal impulse response at the contact surface.

### 4.5 Physics Experiment Design (Section 7.5)

| Parameter | Primary MVP Experiment — Mode A: Surface Dissipation on Ramp & Runout | Secondary MVP Experiment — Mode B: Off-Center Angular Impulse on Flat Deck |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Independent Variable** | Surface `Physics.Matter` on the Real Twin Sphere: **Ice** ($\mu=0.03, \mu_r=0.005, e=0.92$), **Steel** ($\mu=0.35, \mu_r=0.06, e=0.68$), or **Rubber** ($\mu=0.85, \mu_r=0.20, e=0.45$). | Radial impact offset vector $\vec{r}$ on the Real Twin Sphere: **Center** ($\vec{r} = (0,0,0)\text{ cm}$), **Top Rim** ($\vec{r} = (0, +2.5, 0)\text{ cm}$), or **Side Rim** ($\vec{r} = (0, 0, +2.5)\text{ cm}$). |

| **What the Two Models Exhibit** | **Ideal Twin (Cyan):** Slides frictionlessly without rolling ($\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$) and rebounds elastically ($e = 1.0$) off the end bumper. **Real Twin (Amber):** Spins up on the surface ($\vec{\omega} \neq \vec{0}$), loses speed to rolling friction, and rebounds with reduced speed ($e < 1.0$). | **Ideal Twin (Cyan):** Translates purely along $+X$ with zero spin ($\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$). **Real Twin (Amber):** Translates along $+X$ while spinning rapidly ($\vec{\omega} = I^{-1}(\vec{r} \times \vec{J})$); upon hitting the bumper or surface, spin couples with friction to deflect its path. |
| **Required Telemetry** | Spatial divergence $|\Delta \vec{x}|$ ($\text{cm}$), linear speeds $|\vec{v}_{\text{ideal}}|$ vs. $|\vec{v}_{\text{real}}|$ ($\text{cm/s}$), spin rate $|\vec{\omega}_{\text{real}}|$ ($\text{rad/s}$), and per-world energy percentages (`Trans %`, `Spin %`, `Est. Loss %`). | 3D Cross-Product Triad arrows ($\vec{r}$ in $\text{cm}$, $\vec{J}$ in $\text{N}\cdot\text{s}$, $\Delta \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J}$ in $\text{N}\cdot\text{cm}\cdot\text{s}$), post-launch spin rate $|\vec{\omega}_{\text{real}}|$ ($\text{rad/s}$), and lateral/vertical path deflection ($\text{cm}$). |
| **Valid Scientific Conclusion** | Surface friction and inelastic restitution partition mechanical energy into rotational kinetic energy ($\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$) and non-conservative dissipation, reducing translational range and rebound velocity. | An impulse applied at offset $\vec{r}$ from the center of mass imparts both linear momentum ($\Delta \vec{p} = \vec{J}$) and angular momentum ($\Delta \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J}$) orthogonal to both $\vec{r}$ and $\vec{J}$. |
| **Communicated Limitations** | `Physics.Matter` uses simplified product coefficients and empirical rolling/spinning damping rather than microscopic tribology or thermal heat transfer. | Discrete rigid-body solver step (`120 Hz`) approximates instantaneous impulse over one $8.33\text{ ms}$ timestep. |

### 4.7 Visual and Interaction Design (Section 7.7)

All visual and spatial interaction specifications strictly follow [Snap's Positioning & Sizing Content](https://developers.snap.com/spectacles/best-practices/design-for-spectacles/positioning-sizing-content) and [Design Best Practices for Spectacles](https://developers.snap.com/spectacles/best-practices/design-for-spectacles/design-best-practices):

1. **Scene Composition & Binocular Overlap Compliance:**
   - The Spectacles camera sits at $(0, 0, 0)$ with a slightly downward resting boresight angle.
   - `WorkbenchAnchor` is positioned at **`(0, -18, -90)` cm** (mid-field desktop distance). At $Z = -90\text{ cm}$, the binocular overlap region provides approximately $42\text{ cm}$ of horizontal width and $62\text{ cm}$ of vertical height.
   - The physics track spans **$32\text{ cm}$ horizontally ($X \in [-16, +16]\text{ cm}$)**, leaving generous $5\text{ cm}$ safety margins on both the left and right edges so objects never enter the non-overlapping monocular side zones.
2. **Spatial Hierarchy & Parallel Twin Lanes:**
   - **Back Lane (`Z_local = -4.0 cm` inside `WorkbenchAnchor`, i.e., $Z = -94\text{ cm}$):** `World_Ideal_Alpha` — Cyan translucent track rail and Cyan wireframe/Fresnel sphere (`#00F0FF`).
   - **Front Lane (`Z_local = +4.0 cm` inside `WorkbenchAnchor`, i.e., $Z = -86\text{ cm}$):** `World_Real_Beta` — Dark slate track rail with high-contrast $2\text{ cm}$ tick marks and solid metallic/PBR sphere whose color reflects the active material preset:
     - **Ice Preset:** Crystalline Sky Blue (`#7CE8FF`)
     - **Steel Preset:** High-Luminance Warm Gold (`#FFD166`)
     - **Rubber Preset:** High-Visibility Coral Amber (`#FF6B4A`)
   - **Telemetry Readout Banner (`Y_local = +14 cm`, `Z_local = 0 cm`):** A single dark-backed occluding pill panel behind high-contrast white/cyan/amber text (`Provide a background behind text to maintain legibility against varying real-world colors` per [Design Best Practices](https://developers.snap.com/spectacles/best-practices/design-for-spectacles/design-best-practices)).
3. **Desk-Edge Control Bezel Placement & Target Sizing:**
   - Placed at **`(0, -25, -72)` cm**, tilted upward toward the user at **$-30^\circ$ pitch** so the user can glance down slightly without steep neck angles.
   - Contains **at most 6 primary controls** arranged in a clean 2-row or segmented bar (well below the 7-element cognitive comfort limit in [Design Best Practices](https://developers.snap.com/spectacles/best-practices/design-for-spectacles/design-best-practices)):
     1. `Btn_ModeToggle` (*"Mode: Ramp / Spin"*)
     2. `Btn_Matter_Ice` (*"Ice"*)
     3. `Btn_Matter_Steel` (*"Steel"*)
     4. `Btn_Matter_Rubber` (*"Rubber"*)
     5. `Toggle_BulletTime` (*"4x Slow-Mo"*)
     6. `Btn_LaunchReset` (*"Launch / Reset"*)
   - Plus a compact secondary row for deterministic target/offset stepping (`Btn_OffsetCycle`, `Btn_TargetCycle`) so every state is reachable via simple button triggers as well as 3D dragging.
   - **Interactive Target Sizing:** At $Z = -72\text{ cm}$, the recommended button collider size ($2^\circ \text{ to } 3^\circ$ angular size) is **$3.5\text{ cm to }4.5\text{ cm}$** with **$\ge 1.5\text{ cm}$ spacing** between adjacent colliders ([Positioning & Sizing Content](https://developers.snap.com/spectacles/best-practices/design-for-spectacles/positioning-sizing-content)).
4. **3D Vector Semantics & Readability:**
   - **Applied Linear Impulse ($\vec{J}$):** Snap Electric Yellow (`#FFFC00`), shaft radius $0.35\text{ cm}$, cone tip radius $0.9\text{ cm}$.
   - **Moment Arm Offset ($\vec{r}$):** Pure White (`#FFFFFF`), shaft radius $0.25\text{ cm}$.
   - **Angular Impulse ($\Delta \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{J}$):** Vibrant Magenta (`#FF2A85`), shaft radius $0.35\text{ cm}$, protruding orthogonally from the impact point.
   - **Contact Normal Impulse ($\vec{J}_n$):** Luminous Cyan (`#00F0FF`), appearing at `contact.position` during impact.
   - **Hypothesis Target Ring:** Torus/ring of outer radius $4.5\text{ cm}$ in Emerald Green (`#00FF66`), turning Bright Gold/Green on hit and Amber-Red on miss.

### 19.2 Critical Self-Critique of This Plan

#### 1. The Three Weakest Assumptions in This Plan
1. **Weakest Assumption #1 — Behavior of `BodyComponent.addForceAt(force, offset, mode)` Under World vs. Local Coordinates (Question U1):**
   While the official [BodyComponent API documentation](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/api/lens-scripting/classes/Built-In.BodyComponent.html) documents `addForceAt(force, offset, mode)` as applying force *"at a point offset from the object's origin, effectively generating torque"*, physics bindings in 3D engines occasionally differ on whether `offset` is passed as a relative vector $\vec{r} = \vec{x}_{\text{hit}} - \vec{x}_{\text{cm}}$ or a world-space point $\vec{x}_{\text{hit}}$.
   - *Built-In Mitigation:* Phase 6 Experiment 6.3 tests `addForceAt` immediately in the Logger. If `addForceAt` shows any coordinate-space ambiguity, calling `realBody.addForce(J, ForceMode.Impulse)` paired with `realBody.addTorque(r.cross(J), ForceMode.Impulse)` is mathematically 100% equivalent by the Newton-Euler rigid-body theorem and completely eliminates any offset-frame ambiguity!
2. **Weakest Assumption #2 — Compound Seam Smoothness Between `RampCollider` and `DeckFloor` in `World_Ideal_Alpha` and `World_Real_Beta`:**
   When a sphere rolls down a tilted `BoxShape` (`RampCollider` at $-22^\circ$) onto a horizontal `BoxShape` (`DeckFloor`), the geometric crease where the two boxes meet can cause an artificial upward normal bounce if the edges do not overlap cleanly.
   - *Built-In Mitigation:* Phase 5 Failure Recovery specifies overlapping the bottom edge of `RampCollider` slightly into `DeckFloor` (or adding a thin $11^\circ$ bevel transition plate, or launching horizontally along the flat `DeckFloor` with a sloped bumper at the far end).
3. **Weakest Assumption #3 — 3D Manipulation Ergonomics in a 2D Laptop Preview Window (Phase 9):**
   Without physical Spectacles hardware, all hand interactions are driven via a 2D mouse in the Lens Studio Preview panel. Free 3D dragging of multiple nearby handles (`ForceVectorHandle`, `ImpactOffsetPin`, `HypothesisTargetRing`) can result in accidental raycast overlap if handles sit along the same line of sight from the camera.
   - *Built-In Mitigation:* Every 3D parameter (Mode, Material, Offset $\vec{r}$, Target Ring position $X$, and Launch/Reset) is **also** controllable via large, non-overlapping buttons on `DeskEdgeBezel_UI` (`Btn_ModeToggle`, `Btn_OffsetCycle`, `Btn_TargetCycle`, `Btn_LaunchReset`). Even if you never touch a 3D drag handle during your video or LEAF tests, 100% of the product works smoothly via the Desk-Edge Bezel.
